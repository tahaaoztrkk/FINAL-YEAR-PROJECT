\section{Abbreviation expansion with language models}
\label{sec:rw-abbreviation}

The work closest to ours is the SpeakFaster project at Google. \citet{cai2022context} proposed
abbreviating phrases aggressively, mainly to the first letter of each word, and expanding the
abbreviation into full-phrase options with a large language model, using the previous turn of the
conversation as context. On four public English conversation datasets, a 64-billion-parameter model
expanded more than 70\% of phrases with abbreviations of up to ten characters exactly, which gave an
effective keystroke saving rate of up to about 77\% on those exact expansions. A single turn of context
more than doubled expansion accuracy compared with no context.

Our scheme S1 is the Turkish counterpart of their word-initial scheme, and hypothesis H-A4 asks whether
their context effect also holds for Turkish. Two differences motivate this project. First, Turkish is
agglutinative: much of a sentence's grammatical meaning, such as negation, tense and person, is carried
by suffixes that a word-initial abbreviation drops \todo{cite a reference on Turkish morphology}; the
schemes S2--S4 test ways of keeping some of that information. Second, our expansion model has 9 billion
parameters and runs on a laptop rather than in the cloud. Their saving rate is computed only over
exact expansions, whereas Study A also charges the selections spent on failed attempts
(Chapter~\ref{ch:study-a}), so the two figures are not directly comparable.
